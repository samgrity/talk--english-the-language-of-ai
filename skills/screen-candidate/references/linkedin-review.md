# LinkedIn Profile Review

Use this file after you have retrieved a LinkedIn profile and need to evaluate what it means for the decision.

## What to evaluate

Evaluate LinkedIn evidence against the three core questions:

1. **Experience fit**: Does the candidate's role/title and experience history match the position requirements?
2. **Company credibility**: Does the employer context indicate a legitimate company?
3. **Employment verification**: Does the profile plausibly match the candidate and claimed company?

## Positive signals

- Current role/title aligns with the position being screened for.
- Current employer matches application company (or clearly related legal/trade entity).
- Experience history shows sustained relevant work, not a one-off.
- Education/certifications reinforce professional credibility when available.
- Location, naming, and profile details are coherent with application data.

## Negative signals

- Current role appears unrelated to the position (non-relevant support/administrative only).
- Claimed company does not appear in current role or recent history.
- Experience indicates one-off or occasional adjacent activity only.
- Profile appears sparse/incomplete with little evidence of real professional activity.
- Material inconsistencies across name, location, role, dates, or company identity.
- Indicators of misrepresentation or identity mismatch (for example a clearly different person/profile context).

## How to use LinkedIn evidence in decisions

- Strong profile alignment can resolve ambiguity for personal/shared email cases.
- Weak or conflicting profile evidence should usually move to `recommend_follow_up` unless disqualifying evidence is clear.
- If profile evidence clearly contradicts claimed role/company identity, `recommend_decline` can be appropriate.
- If LinkedIn evidence materially informed the recommendation, inline the exact LinkedIn URL in checklist lines and in `Rationale` so recruiters can see how it was used.

## If LinkedIn retrieval fails

- Retrieval failure is not positive evidence and should not be used to justify advancement.
- If employment verification and experience are still not sufficiently proven after LinkedIn failure, prefer `recommend_follow_up`.
- Advance without LinkedIn only when equivalent alternate evidence is strong enough (for example named company-domain email alignment plus credible company/team evidence, published portfolio, certifications, or prior validated correspondence).
- If alternate evidence is weak, conflicting, or incomplete, do not advance.

## Correspondence implications

When LinkedIn evidence is incomplete or conflicting, request one or two precise clarifying items (for example team-page link, updated company email, or role verification source) rather than a broad questionnaire.
